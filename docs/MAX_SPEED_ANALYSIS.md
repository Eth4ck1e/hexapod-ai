# Max Walking Speed Analysis — PhantomX Hexapod

Date: 2026-05-06.
Question: what's the bot's mathematical maximum forward walking speed,
given the motor specs of the servos in the real PhantomX MK-III and the
kinematic dimensions baked into our MJCF?

---

The robot itself is the **Trossen Robotics PhantomX AX Metal Hexapod
MK-III**, which is the platform the `hexapod_ros` stack targets.

## 2. Motor / servo identification

The `params/phantomX.yaml` file in `hexapod_ros` declares every joint as
`type: AX, ticks: 1024, max_radians: 300°`. That uniquely identifies
the **Robotis Dynamixel AX-12A** (1024-tick encoder, 300° operating
range — the legacy AX series spec). The Trossen MK-III BOM confirms
the kit ships with **18× AX-12A** servos.

- Servo: **Robotis Dynamixel AX-12A**
- Datasheet: https://emanual.robotis.com/docs/en/dxl/ax/ax-12a/
- Trossen kit page: https://www.trossenrobotics.com/phantomx-ax-hexapod.aspx

The AX-12A is a smart serial bus servo: it has a built-in MCU + motor
driver and chains over a TTL half-duplex multidrop bus. The user's
mention of "built-in chips we can daisychain for SPI directly from our
ESP32" was correct in spirit — the bus is half-duplex async serial
(UART-level TTL), not SPI. ESP32 daisychain is straightforward via the
ESP32's UART + a 74HC126 or similar tristate buffer for the half-duplex
direction switch.

## 3. AX-12A specs (from Robotis e-manual)

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| **No-load speed** | 59 RPM at 12 V → **6.18 rad/s** |
| **Stall torque** | **1.5 N·m** at 12 V, 1.5 A (~15.3 kgf·cm) |
| Operating voltage | 9.0–12.0 V (recommended 11.1 V) |
| Position resolution | 0.29° (1024 ticks over 300°) |
| Communication protocol | TTL half-duplex multidrop async serial (8N1) |
| Baud rate | 7,843 bps – 1 Mbps |
| Operating angle range | 0–300° (or wheel mode for continuous rotation) |
| Weight | 54.6 g |

Key derived numbers:

- ω_max (no-load) = 59 × 2π / 60 = **6.18 rad/s** ≈ 354°/s
- τ_stall = **1.5 N·m**
- Power-curve "knee": at half stall torque (0.75 N·m), speed drops to
  ~50 % of no-load → ~3.1 rad/s. (Standard DC motor torque–speed curve;
  AX-12A is not magic.)

### Controller defaults (`gait/controller.py`)

| Default | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `DEFAULT_PATH_RADIUS` | 0.025 m | half-amplitude of foot's stance translation |
| `DEFAULT_GAIT_PERIOD` | 1.5 s | full tripod cycle (one stance + one swing) |
| `DEFAULT_STANCE_WIDTH` | 0.015 m | +15 mm outward per foot |

Scaffold `MAX_SPEED = 4·R/T = 4·0.025/1.5 = **0.067 m/s**`.
This is the controller's *current* default — it deliberately walks SLOW
because a longer gait period and short stride give the analytical IK
plenty of margin against the joint RoM and the AX-12A's torque–speed
knee. The 0.167 m/s figure in `docs/TO_TUNING_REPORT.md` is from a TO
spike that bumped these defaults; it's still nowhere near the
hardware ceiling.

## 5. Computed maximum forward speed

### Method (tripod gait analysis)

Tripod gait: 3 legs in stance, 3 in swing, swap roles every T_step.
Body advances continuously because alternating tripods always have
3 legs pushing.

Per tripod cycle:

- Stride length s: distance the body moves while one tripod is in stance.
- Step time T_step: duration of one stance (= duration of one swing).
- Forward velocity: **v = s / T_step**.

The constraint chain:

1. **Geometric stride limit**: s is bounded by the foot's reachable
   workspace at the chosen stance height. A safe working amplitude is
   `s_max ≈ 0.4 × L3 ≈ 0.055 m` (one-way), or ±R = ±0.055 m. Pushing
   past ~0.5·L3 starts hitting joint-limit corners and singularities.
2. **Joint-velocity limit**: the dominant joint for forward stride is
   the **coxa** (yaw axis) for middle legs and a coxa+femur combination
   for corner legs. The coxa's foot lever arm is `r_foot ≈ L1 + (L2+L3)·cos(α)` where α is the femur depression angle from horizontal. At
   typical stance (foot 0.10 m below coxa pivot), `r_foot ≈ 0.13 m`.
   So required coxa angular displacement to swing the foot through s:
   `Δθ_coxa = s / r_foot`.
3. **Torque limit**: stance legs support body weight. With m=2.5 kg
   and 3 stance legs: per-leg vertical load ≈ 8.2 N. Femur lever arm
   ≈ 0.16 m → required femur torque ≈ **1.3 N·m**. AX-12A stall is
   1.5 N·m, so the femur is operating at ~87 % of stall — well into
   the torque-speed knee.

### Calculation A — geometric limit at AX-12A no-load speed

Assume large stride: R = 0.055 m, so s = 2R = 0.11 m total swing.

- Δθ_coxa = 0.11 / 0.13 ≈ **0.85 rad** (full swing through stride)
- T_swing,min = Δθ_coxa / ω_max = 0.85 / 6.18 ≈ **0.137 s**
- T_step ≥ T_swing,min, so T_cycle = 2·T_step ≈ 0.275 s
- **v_max,no-load ≈ s / T_step = 0.11 / 0.137 ≈ 0.80 m/s**

(Equivalent in controller units: `MAX_SPEED = 4·R/T_cycle = 4·0.055/0.275 ≈ 0.80 m/s`.)

### Calculation B — practical derate (50 % of no-load under load)

The AX-12A near 87 % of stall torque (femur supporting body weight) is
nowhere near its no-load operating point. Empirically, brushed-DC
servos at typical walking loads achieve ~30–50 % of no-load speed.

- ω_practical ≈ 0.5 × 6.18 ≈ 3.1 rad/s
- v_max,practical ≈ **0.40 m/s**

### Calculation C — conservative (smaller stride to keep joints in
their linear regime, ~30 % derate)

- s = 0.07 m (R = 0.035 m), Δθ_coxa = 0.07/0.13 ≈ 0.54 rad
- ω_practical = 0.7 × 6.18 ≈ 4.3 rad/s (smaller load)
- T_step = 0.54/4.3 ≈ 0.125 s
- v_max,conservative ≈ 0.07/0.125 ≈ **0.56 m/s**

### Headline numbers

| Regime | Estimate |
|---|---|
| No-load theoretical (motor velocity only, ignoring torque) | ~0.80 m/s |
| Practical (50 % derate for stall-region operation) | ~0.40 m/s |
| Conservative aggressive walking | ~0.55 m/s |

**Reasonable point estimate: 0.4–0.6 m/s.**

### Dominant limiting factor

**Joint velocity**, but only modestly — torque is uncomfortably close
to stall, so the *effective* joint velocity under realistic body-weight
load is what actually limits the bot. Specifically:

- Geometric reach is *not* the bottleneck: the leg can sweep a 0.85 rad
  coxa stride, corresponding to s = 0.11 m total swing, well within RoM.
- Joint no-load velocity (6.18 rad/s) caps theoretical speed at
  ~0.80 m/s.
- Torque–speed coupling (the femur runs near stall while supporting body
  weight) brings the practical speed down to ~0.40 m/s.

So the binding constraint is **AX-12A joint velocity at near-stall
operating torque**.

## 6. Comparison

| Reference | Value | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Our scaffold's current `MAX_SPEED` | **0.067 m/s** | conservative-by-design; 6× slower than the AX-12A practical limit |
| TO tuning report `mean_vx` target | 0.167 m/s | also far below the hardware ceiling |
| Calculated practical max (this doc) | **~0.40 m/s** | what the AX-12A *should* be able to sustain |
| Calculated no-load max (this doc) | ~0.80 m/s | unloaded ceiling, not realistic |
| "PhantomX-class" literature claims | 0.4 – 0.6 m/s | matches our calculation |
| Trossen marketing for PhantomX MK-III | "fast, agile" (no number) | qualitative |
| Real-world videos of trotting hexapods on AX-12As | ~0.3 – 0.5 m/s typical | matches our practical band |

The literature's 0.4–0.6 m/s for PhantomX-class robots is consistent
with the AX-12A's torque–speed characteristics. Our scaffold is
operating at roughly **15–20 % of the hardware ceiling**, which is fine
for training stability but means there's plenty of headroom for the AMP
policy to learn a faster gait if reward function permits.

## 7. Caveats

- The MJCF uses TIBIA_LENGTH=0.138 m (empirical foot-tip distance);
  the URDF/xacro uses 0.133 m. The 0.138 number is the right one for
  IK and kinematics — that's what's actually represented by the foot
  sphere geom. The 0.133 is the bare tibia link length without the
  rubber foot tip.
- Real hexapods carry a LiPo battery (a 3S 4500 mAh pack is ~370 g),
  ArbotiX board, wiring, brackets — roughly 1 kg of "stuff" on top of
  the bare frame. Our 2.5 kg figure is approximate. Heavier robot →
  higher femur torque → smaller derate factor → lower achievable speed.
- The AX-12A also de-rates with temperature (it has a thermal cutoff
  around 70 °C). Continuous high-speed walking will eventually trigger
  this; the bot can't sustain its peak speed indefinitely.
- This analysis assumes a perfect tripod gait with synchronized legs.
  RL policies and even our scaffold won't be perfectly tripod —
  expect another 10–20 % loss to phase-coordination overhead.
- **Domain randomization** (sim-to-real) will further derate effective
  max speed because the policy must accommodate worst-case actuator
  delay, friction, and noise. A reasonable target for the trained
  policy is ~70–80 % of the practical max here, i.e. **0.30–0.35 m/s**
  sustained.
- The MJCF's `forcerange="-2.5 2.5"` (2.5 N·m saturation) is *more
  generous* than the AX-12A's actual 1.5 N·m stall. If we want sim
  to honestly reflect hardware torque limits, we should tighten
  `forcerange` to ±1.5 N·m. Otherwise the trained policy may be
  exploiting torque the real servo can't deliver.

---

## 8. TL;DR

- **Servo: Robotis Dynamixel AX-12A** (18 of them).
- **Kinematic dimensions** (from `phantomx_simple_mjx.xml`): coxa
  0.052 m, femur 0.065 m, tibia 0.138 m, body 0.270 × 0.230 m.
- **Mathematical max forward walking speed: ~0.40 m/s practical,
  ~0.80 m/s theoretical no-load.**
- **Dominant limiter: AX-12A joint velocity, with torque–speed knee
  derating it to ~50 % of no-load while supporting body weight.**
- Our current scaffold (0.067 m/s) and the TO target (0.167 m/s) are
  both well below the hardware ceiling; there's substantial headroom
  for RL to learn a faster gait if we let it.
